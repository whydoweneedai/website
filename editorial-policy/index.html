Editorial standards

How we create and review our content.

Our goal is useful, understandable guidance that helps readers make their own decisions about AI tools and automation.

Practical value comes first

We choose topics based on problems small businesses actually face: repetitive work, tool selection, process readiness, cost, risk, and implementation. We avoid publishing pages simply to repeat information already available elsewhere.

Research and verification

When an article makes a factual claim that can change over time, our standard is to check current primary documentation or other reliable sources. We distinguish general guidance from claims that depend on a specific product, plan, law, or policy.

How we use AI

AI tools may assist with research organization, outlines, drafting, editing, or quality checks. A person remains responsible for topic selection, publication decisions, and final review. Our standard is not to publish raw, unreviewed AI output or mass-generated pages created only to target search terms.

Testing and experience

When we describe a tool as tested, show a workflow, or report a result from an experiment, we aim to explain what was actually tested and the limits of that test. A product that works for one workflow is not automatically the right choice for every business.

Advertising and commercial relationships

The site may display advertising. Advertising placement does not determine article topics, recommendations, or conclusions. If a future article includes a paid sponsorship, affiliate relationship, or another material commercial connection, our standard is to disclose it clearly on that content.

Corrections and updates

If we learn that a material fact is wrong or outdated, we aim to correct it promptly. Readers can flag potential errors through the contact page. Significant content updates may be reflected in an article's modified date.

High-stakes topics

Our content is educational and is not a substitute for legal, medical, financial, cybersecurity, employment, or other professional advice. Higher-risk uses of AI deserve stronger review, controls, and appropriate professional guidance.

The short version

Be useful, be clear about what we know, disclose how content is produced, correct mistakes, and do not let advertising write the answer.
