## What works about the reference

- **A clear thesis upfront.** An opening editorial take interprets the news, instead of repeating a generic AI introduction. The issue describes its structure as a prediction, curated stories, and a closing vision (pages 1–2).
- **Selective, cross-topic curation.** It groups short news briefs under AI, robotics, hardware, gaming, and streaming. Not all topics need coverage every week. Stories earn inclusion by informing the thesis (pages 3–10).
- **Facts plus judgment.** Briefs present a specific product or industry development, then suggest why the underlying business move matters. The interpretation is more important than paraphrasing an announcement (pages 3–7).
- **Conversational voice.** The prose is direct, memorable, occasionally playful, and willing to challenge the obvious headline. Do not invent personal experiences or borrow the original author's signature lines (pages 4, 6, 8).
- **Synthesis rather than a recap.** The closing section connects the stories while explicitly considering a plausible skeptical case (pages 11–12).
- **Scannability.** Concise sections, specific headlines, short paragraphs, linked references, and selective visuals create a readable long issue. Do not copy the newsletter's branding, imagery, visual assets, or sponsor material.

## How Why Do We Need AI should adapt it

News-driven articles can follow this flexible structure:

1. **The headline:** a concrete development or tension, not "AI is changing everything."
2. **Our take:** one defensible, original judgment; any prediction is clearly labeled as a forecast and has a possible falsification signal.
3. **What happened:** 1–5 sourced developments, selected for relevance rather than quota. Distinguish announced, preview, available, and tested capabilities.
4. **Why it matters:** an operational implication for a reader. Not every story needs an identical section template.
5. **What to watch or do:** an act/watch/wait matrix, short decision note, comparison, or concrete test, based on the evidence. Avoid forcing a named scoring framework into every story.
6. **The connection:** a closing synthesis that acknowledges the best alternative interpretation.

This is one format in the broader editorial mix, not a replacement for practical evergreen guides.

## Attribution and verification

- Research fresh facts against primary vendor documentation, official announcements, or strong independent reporting; link to sources near the supporting claims.
- Clearly separate facts, vendor assertions, editorial interpretation, and prediction.
- State the source publication date and product availability as of article publication; do not present previews as generally available.
- Do not claim first-hand tests, personal usage, customer outcomes, interviews, or quotes that have not actually occurred.
- Do not repeat unsupported numbers or dramatic claims from the reference.
- Do not copy protected language, artwork, slogans, or substantial passages from the newsletter.
- Keep specific, honest limitations and a useful takeaway for small-business readers.

## Editor's decision

A publication that only produces "five tips" articles lacks its own point of view. Alternate evergreen workflow help with selective technology analysis, and make every story earn its place. The editorial identity is thoughtful, accessible analysis—not maximal hype.
