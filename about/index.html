About us

AI should solve a real problem—not become another project.

Why Do We Need AI helps small-business owners figure out where AI and automation can actually save time, improve work, or remove repetitive tasks.

Ross C, editor

Ross C is the editor of Why Do We Need AI. The site publishes guides, checklists, worked examples, and documented tests to help small-business owners decide where AI or automation fits their work.

Ross is responsible for the site's editorial direction and publication decisions. Articles identify their author or reviewer and explain when examples are illustrative or a test uses invented scenarios. Our editorial policy describes research, AI assistance, review, and corrections.

For a correction, question, or article suggestion, contact Ross through the site.

Why this site exists

AI advice is often written for technical teams or built around whatever tool is newest. Small businesses usually need a different answer: what is worth trying, what can wait, what could go wrong, and how do you know whether a tool is helping?

We focus on those practical questions. The goal is not to convince every business to use more AI. It is to help people make better decisions about when AI is useful and when a simpler process or standard automation is the better choice.

What we publish

  • Plain-English guides for choosing and testing AI tools.
  • Checklists for evaluating readiness, risk, cost, and workflow fit.
  • Practical automation ideas focused on measurable business problems.
  • Tools such as the AI ReadyScore assessment that help identify a sensible starting point.

How we approach recommendations

We start with the work that needs to be done, not with a product. We favor small tests using real examples, clear success measures, human review, and an exit plan. When a workflow carries more risk, we say so.

Advertising does not determine what we write or the conclusions in our guides. See our editorial policy for more about research, AI assistance, corrections, and commercial relationships.

Our standard

If a recommendation cannot be explained in plain English, tested on real work, and measured against the current process, it is probably not ready to become business advice.
