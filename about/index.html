About us

Why AI matters, in plain English

Why Do We Need AI helps small-business owners figure out where AI and automation can actually save time, improve work, or remove repetitive tasks.

What we're for

Most small-business owners have heard that AI will change how they work, but few have been shown what it's actually good for. We try to close that gap. Each article picks one real job, such as reading a contract, drafting a follow-up email, entering invoices into a spreadsheet or deciding whether a pilot is worth keeping, and shows how AI can help with it, what it gets wrong and what to check.

We're not here to sell AI for its own sake. When a simple rule, a checklist or a standard automation does the job better, we say so.

Who runs it

Why Do We Need AI is edited by Ross C. Ross sets the site's editorial direction and approves every article before it's published. For a correction, question or article idea, use the contact page or email admin@whydoweneedai.com.

What we publish

  • How-to guides that walk through one task step by step, with the exact prompt we used.
  • Researched recommendations built from each company's own documentation and independent reviews, with links to both.
  • Tests that run an AI tool through a set of cases and report the results, including what went wrong.
  • Checklists and worked examples for deciding where AI fits and whether it's earning its keep.
  • The AI ReadyScore assessment, a short questionnaire that suggests a sensible place to start.

How we make and check articles

  • AI-assisted, human-approved. Articles are researched and drafted with an AI writing assistant. Ross edits, checks and approves each one. A line under every byline says how the piece was made.
  • We say what kind of article it is. A test says what we tested, what happened and its limits. A researched recommendation says it wasn't tested. An illustrative example is labeled as illustrative.
  • Our tests so far use made-up data. The cases are invented, not real customers, businesses or accounts, and each test says so. Where we can, we publish the inputs, the raw outputs and the scoring files as a download, with SHA-256 fingerprints so any later change would show.
  • We don't report time saved unless we timed it. When we didn't time something, the article says so.
  • How-to results come from real runs. When a guide quotes what a chatbot produced, it gives the date of the run and whether we were signed in, and we check the numbers against the source.
  • Facts carry dates. Prices, plan features and settings change, so we say when we checked them.
