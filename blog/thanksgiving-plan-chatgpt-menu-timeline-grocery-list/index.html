Hosting Thanksgiving? One prompt gets you the menu, the timeline and the grocery list

Give ChatGPT four facts about your kitchen and ask for a menu, an hour-by-hour plan and one grocery list sorted by aisle. Then check the turkey against USDA.

Oct 28, 2026•Updated Nov 8, 2026•6-minute read•By Ross C, editor

Researched and drafted with the site's AI writing assistant. Edited, checked, and approved by Ross C. How we work

In this article

The short answer: Paste one detailed prompt into ChatGPT. No account needed. Include your guest count, your dinner time, your oven and burners, and your turkey's weight. You get back a menu, a timeline that works backward from dinner to the day your turkey goes into the fridge to thaw, and one grocery list sorted by aisle. Then check the turkey steps against USDA before you trust them.

Thanksgiving dinner doesn't fall apart over the recipes. It falls apart over the logistics: the turkey that's still frozen on Wednesday night, two dishes that need the oven at different temperatures, the third trip to the store for celery. That's a scheduling problem, and a good one to hand an AI chatbot for a first draft. Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. Start now and you can have a plan before you buy anything.

What you need

Just ChatGPT. Everything here is plain text, so you don't need to upload anything or even sign in. We ran the prompt below without one.

Step 1: Write down four facts

Before you open the chat, write down:

  1. Who's coming: the headcount, kids, and any vegetarian, allergy or other dietary needs.
  2. When you're eating: the actual dinner time.
  3. Your equipment: how many ovens and burners you have, plus anything extra, like a slow cooker.
  4. Your turkey: its weight, and whether it's frozen or fresh.

Leave out the equipment and the chatbot has no way to know you only have one oven.

Step 2: Paste this prompt

Swap in your own details:

I'm hosting Thanksgiving dinner on Thursday, November 26, for 10 people: 8 adults and 2 kids, and one adult is vegetarian. Dinner is at 4 PM. I have one oven, four burners and a slow cooker. I'm buying a 16-pound frozen turkey.

1. Menu: suggest turkey, one vegetarian main, five sides and two desserts. Mark every dish that can be made the day before.
2. Timeline: work backward from 4 PM Thursday. Start with the day the turkey has to move to the fridge to thaw (use USDA's 24 hours per 4 to 5 pounds). Plan oven use so two dishes never need different temperatures at the same time, and say what goes on the stovetop or in the slow cooker instead.
3. Grocery list: one list with quantities for 10 people, sorted by store aisle (produce, meat, dairy and eggs, bakery, baking, canned and dry goods, frozen, drinks). Put salt, pepper, oil, butter and spices I probably own under "Check the pantry first."

Keep it practical. No commentary.

The line about oven temperatures is there to make the chatbot plan around a single oven. Check that it did: no two dishes that need different temperatures should be in the oven at the same time.

Step 3: Check the turkey against USDA, not the chatbot

This is the one step you can't hand off. USDA says to thaw a turkey in the fridge for about 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds. For a 12- to 16-pound bird, that's 3 to 4 days, so a 16-pounder needs to be in the fridge by Sunday, November 22. A turkey is done at 165°F, measured in the thickest part of the breast and the innermost parts of the thigh and wing.

Step 4: Tighten it with follow-ups

Keep going in the same chat:

Two more people are coming and one is gluten-free. Update the menu, the timeline and the grocery list.
Turn the grocery list into a checklist I can paste into my phone's notes app.

What you end up with

We ran the exact prompt above in ChatGPT without an account on October 28, 2026. It returned a full menu with make-ahead notes, a day-by-day timeline from Sunday's thaw to the 4 PM dinner, and a grocery list sorted by aisle. Here is the turkey entry on its menu:

One 16-pound turkey, roasted with onion, lemon, garlic and herbs. Allow roughly 3½–4¼ hours of roasting, plus resting time; use a thermometer rather than time alone.

That's good advice, and it also contains the one number worth catching. USDA's timetable for an unstuffed 14- to 18-pound turkey at 325°F says 3¾ to 4¼ hours, so the menu's low end is 15 minutes short. Use the chatbot for the plan. For the bird itself, check the times against USDA and trust the thermometer, which is what ChatGPT told you to do anyway.

So what?

The work that makes a holiday dinner stressful is coordination: what happens when, in which pan, bought on which trip. That's the work you can hand to a chatbot for a first draft. Run the prompt this weekend, check the thaw date against USDA, and you can go shopping with one list sorted by aisle.
