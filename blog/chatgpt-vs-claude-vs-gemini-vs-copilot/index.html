ChatGPT vs Claude vs Gemini vs Copilot: which one a small business should actually pay for

The AI in your email plan covers most jobs. Here's when Claude or ChatGPT earns $20 a month, and the setting that matters more than the brand.

Published October 29, 2026•Updated October 29, 2026•10-minute read•By Ross C · Researched from vendor documentation and independent reviews, checked Oct 26 2026. We did not run our own tests.

You already pay for email and documents, and that suite now comes with an AI. The real question is whether ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini is worth another $20 a month, and for which jobs. It depends less on which is smartest than on where your work lives, and on a privacy setting most owners never check: on ChatGPT, one thumbs-up can hand a whole conversation to model training.

The short answer: Start with the AI in the email suite you already pay for, and pay $20 more only for a job it can't do. Whichever you use, check the training setting, because on consumer plans that decides what happens to your chats.

Google Workspace: Gemini drafts replies in Gmail on every Business edition. Business Standard can read a long contract in one go.

Microsoft 365: Copilot Chat comes at no extra cost. Copilot inside Outlook, Word and Excel is sold as the paid Copilot add-on, $21 per user a month paid yearly (checked Oct 26 2026; check current price).

Long contracts, without Business Standard: Claude Pro.

Marketing images, or charts from exported data: ChatGPT Plus. Any number you will act on or send to a customer belongs in a spreadsheet formula.

Which one for which job

PCMag and Zapier name ChatGPT best overall; CNET picks Claude for its "consistency." The picks below rest on what you can check: where the tool lives, how much it reads at once, and whether your plan trains on your data.

Customer replies: use the AI in your email

Pick: on Google Workspace, Gemini in Gmail. "Help me write" and "Suggested replies" are on Business Starter, Standard and Plus (Google). On Microsoft 365, the free Copilot Chat is "a web-grounded AI assistant"; Copilot in Outlook, Word and Excel is what the paid add-on sells, at $21 per user a month paid yearly (checked Oct 26 2026; check current price) (Microsoft).

Why: it drafts inside the thread. On Workspace Business editions, Google says chats "won't be reviewed by human reviewers or otherwise used to improve generative AI models" (Google). Microsoft says prompts and responses "aren't used to train foundation models" (Microsoft).

Quotes and estimates: no chatbot should set your price

Pick: prices come from your price sheet or formulas. Let your suite's AI write the scope note and follow-up.

Why: OpenAI itself tells users to "review the generated code, outputs, and assumptions before relying on the result" (OpenAI). We found no independent study showing that any of the four prices jobs more accurately than the others.

Contracts and long documents: context window decides it

Pick: if you're on Workspace Business Standard or Plus, Gemini already handles it. Otherwise, pay for Claude Pro and switch training off.

Why: the plans differ on the context window, how much each can read at once.

PCMag points to Claude's "large context windows" but warns that "its usage limits can be aggressive" (PCMag). None of this is legal advice: read the clause yourself (see the court cases below).

Spreadsheets: formulas for decisions, ChatGPT for exploring

Pick: put any number you'll act on or send in a formula, and have the AI in your spreadsheet app write or explain it. To explore and chart an exported sales file, use ChatGPT Plus: it runs Python on .xlsx and .csv files (OpenAI) and lists interactive tables and charts on Plus (OpenAI).

Why: a formula can be checked line by line. OpenAI says formula complexity can affect how ChatGPT processes a spreadsheet (OpenAI), and Google's =AI function in Sheets returns text only, for the first 350 cells (Google).

Google says Gemini in Sheets can "create formulas that simplify complex calculations" (Google), and Anthropic says Claude builds Excel files "with working formulas" (Anthropic). Both are vendor claims.

Marketing copy and images: images are the real difference

Pick: use your suite's AI for posts and emails. If you need images, get ChatGPT Plus or Google AI Pro.

Why: OpenAI offers image generation on all ChatGPT tiers (OpenAI). Google AI Pro includes Nano Banana Pro, Google's image generation and editing model (Google); PCMag calls Google's image tech "the best we've seen in a chatbot." Anthropic says Claude "doesn't generate photos or illustrations the way image-generation tools do" (Anthropic).

On writing, reviewers split: Zapier's Ryan Kane finds Claude "sounds more natural" (Zapier); CNET says ChatGPT "can adapt to the instructed tone easily."

The twist: the plan, not the brand, decides your privacy

The consumer plans don't all start in the same place.

ChatGPT Free, Plus and Pro: "data sharing is enabled by default." Turn it off under Settings > Data controls > "Improve the model for everyone." Even then, if you give a thumbs-up or thumbs-down, "the entire conversation associated with that feedback may be used to train OpenAI models" (OpenAI).

Claude Free, Pro and Max: Anthropic asks new users to "pick your setting for model training during the signup process" (Anthropic), and its pricing table lists training as "Opt-out" (Anthropic). With training on, chats may be kept up to five years; with it off, 30 days. Thumbs feedback is kept five years (Anthropic).

Consumer Gemini: "If you're 18 or over, Keep Activity is on by default" (Google). With it on, Google uses your activity to improve its services "(including training generative AI models)," with help from human reviewers. Activity auto-deletes after 18 months by default; chats reviewed by humans are kept up to three years (Google).

Business plans don't train by default: ChatGPT Business (OpenAI), Claude Team (Anthropic), Gemini in Workspace Business editions (Google) and Copilot (Microsoft). One exception: Copilot's web searches go to Bing under the Microsoft Services Agreement, outside your business data terms.

How they go wrong

  • They all make things up. Damien Charlotin's AI Hallucination Cases database listed 2,217 cases where a court found someone relied on AI-made-up content, as of Oct 25 2026. It doesn't say which tools were used.
  • News answers. In an EBU/BBC study published October 2025, 45% of AI answers about news had at least one significant issue: Gemini 76%, Copilot 37%, ChatGPT 36% (EBU report). It covered news questions only, didn't test Claude, and used year-old versions.

Our take

Default: use the AI in the suite you already pay for: Gemini on Workspace (Business Standard if you read long documents), or Copilot Chat on Microsoft 365.

Add Claude Pro only for long contracts your suite can't read in one go.

Add ChatGPT Plus only if you want marketing images or to explore and chart exported data. Turn training off; never rate a chat with customer details.

Add the Copilot add-on only if you want Copilot drafting inside Outlook, Word and Excel.

Keep every number you act on in a formula. Don't pay for "better writing"; reviewers don't agree on it.
